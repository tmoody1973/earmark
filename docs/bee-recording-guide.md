# What to record on your Bee — a guide for Earmark

This guide is for Tarik, written against how Earmark actually listens today (Oct 16, 2026). Earmark hears your Bee two ways: **ambient** (it reads your normal recorded conversations on its own) and **"Earmark that"** (a voice note you make on purpose). Ambient catches things you didn't plan to save; the voice note is the sure thing.

> **Consent first.** Tell people when your Bee is on — guests, co-hosts, friends at dinner. Earmark keeps only first names and a 25-word excerpt, but the people talking deserve to know.

---

## 1. The sure thing: "Earmark that" voice notes

Start a **voice note** in Bee (the way you normally record a note — not a regular conversation). Open with the trigger, then say **who** and **what**.

**The pattern:** "Earmark that, **[first name]**'s **[the specific thing]**[, one detail]."

### Recommendations someone gave you

| Type | Say this |
|---|---|
| Restaurant / place | "Earmark that, **Ann**'s ramen spot — **Kawa Ramen** on Brady Street." |
| Album | "Earmark that, **Marcus** says listen to the new **Sault** record, *Acts of Faith*." |
| Song | "Earmark that, **Dana**'s song pick — **'Little Dark Age' by MGMT**." |
| Artist | "Earmark that, **Jordan** told me to check out **Khruangbin**." |
| Book | "Earmark that, **Priya** recommends ***Range* by David Epstein**." |
| Podcast | "Earmark that, **Sam**'s podcast tip — ***The Rewatchables***." |
| Film / TV | "Earmark that, **Lena** says watch ***Shōgun*** on Hulu." |
| Anything else | "Earmark that, **Chris** says try the **Notion calendar app**." |

### Your own ideas

| Say this |
|---|
| "Earmark that — an idea for a **This Bites** episode on Milwaukee's late-night taco trucks." |
| "Earmark this: a **Rhythm Lab** segment pairing jazz records with the neighborhoods they were recorded in." |
| "Earmark that — newsletter idea for **The Intersection**: how public radio stations are using AI for archives." |

### Why this wording works

- **"Earmark that" must come first.** Filler before it is fine ("Um, okay, earmark that…", "Hey Bee, earmark that…"). Said at the end or in the middle, it's ignored on purpose.
- **Say the first name.** Bee can't tell who's speaking — every voice is labelled "Unknown". The name in your note is how Earmark knows who to thank. Without a name, the tip is saved as "from someone, not sure who".
- **Name the actual thing.** "Kawa Ramen", not "that place she mentioned". Earmark can only look up and credit what it hears.
- **One thing per note.** Two tips in one note become one item.
- **Your own name won't be credited.** "Earmark that, Tarik's idea…" works fine — Earmark never lists you as the recommender of your own tips.

### What doesn't work

| ❌ Said this | Why |
|---|---|
| "Ann's ramen spot on Brady Street — earmark that." | Trigger at the end is ignored. |
| "Earmark that." (nothing after) | Nothing to save. |
| "Earmark that and also the book Marcus mentioned." | Two items; only one is saved. |
| "Earmark that place she told me about." | No name, no specific thing — Earmark can't look it up or credit anyone. |

## 2. Ambient: what Earmark catches in normal conversation

You don't do anything special — wear the Bee and talk. After a conversation ends and Bee writes its summary, Earmark reads it in short slices and keeps what looks like an idea or a tip.

### Tips other people give you — it catches these best when someone…

- **Tells you directly:** "You *have* to try the butter pecan at Kopp's." · "You'd love Sault's new record."
- **Raves about a specific thing to you:** "Kawa Ramen has the best broth in the city."
- **Passes along someone else's tip:** "Dana told me to check out *Range*."

### Ideas — it catches these best when someone says…

- "What if we…" · "We could do a piece on…" · "An idea for a segment / story / show / product…"
- "We should have [artist] on the show." (counts as a segment idea)
- Ideas from colleagues count too — the garden is "ideas you keep hearing", not only ideas you said first (decision 003).

### What it deliberately skips

- **Ads and broadcast audio** — sponsor reads, radio spots, station IDs ("88.9…"), a podcast playing in the room. Earmark asks "is this a broadcast?" and drops those. (Two people *talking about* an ad they heard is still a real tip.)
- **Jokes and sarcasm** — "You should quit and move to Mars."
- **"Don't bother with X."** — telling someone *not* to try something.
- **Your own on-air recommendations** — "Folks, you need to hear the whole record." (Your recs to listeners aren't tips to you.)
- **Errands and plans** — "I should call the plumber."

### Known gaps (being fixed in M2)

- **A tip to the whole room** ("Everyone should read *Range*") may be dropped right now. If it matters, back it up with a voice note.
- **Work chatter** (tool names, organizations) sometimes slips in as a "tip". Those wait in "needs review"; M2's evaluation tunes this.
- **Who said it**: from ambient audio alone, Earmark usually can't tell who gave a tip. That's the biggest reason to use "Earmark that" for anything you want credited.

## 3. Planting the demo: the idea that blooms at its third mention

The lead moment in the demo video is a real idea reaching its **third mention** and blooming while you're talking. That needs three genuine mentions on three occasions.

**Pick two ideas you actually care about** (two, in case one doesn't cluster cleanly). Good candidates already showed up in your Oct 6–14 conversations: *a CRM for public radio*, *linking radio stories to locations in an app*.

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Fri Oct 16** | Mention idea A and idea B once each, naturally, in a conversation with someone ("I keep thinking we should build a CRM for public radio…"). |
| **Sat Oct 17 or Sun Oct 18** | Mention each again, in a different conversation, from a slightly different angle ("…it could track listener relationships the way sales tools do"). |
| **Record the bloom (Oct 19–21)** | Mention idea A a third time **with the garden open on your phone or laptop** and the screen recorder running. |

**Tips**
- Same idea, different words is fine — that's what Earmark is built to recognise. Keep the *core* the same (same subject, same kind of thing to make).
- Say it in conversation with someone, not alone into the Bee — ambient conversations are what the compost reads.
- Don't over-plant: three real mentions beat ten forced ones. The PRD says plainly the demo must be real.

## 4. Demo-day checklist (beats from the PRD video script)

| Beat | What to record |
|---|---|
| **Live bloom (0:15–0:45)** | Third mention of your planted idea while the garden is on screen. |
| **"Earmark that" (0:45–1:05)** | A friend (e.g. "Ann") recommends a restaurant; you tap Bee: *"Earmark that, Ann's ramen spot — Kawa Ramen on Brady Street."* It should land in seconds with her name. |
| **Photo / song (1:05–1:20)** | (M5 feature) Photograph an album a friend holds up; or use the Shazam shortcut on a song playing nearby — **don't let copyrighted music play in the video's audio**. |
| **Location nudge (1:20–1:35)** | (M5 feature) Days later, walk near the restaurant; your watch buzzes. |

**Never** include music, Apple Music previews, or radio audio in the demo video's soundtrack — contest rule.

## 5. Quick reference card

```
EARMARK A TIP        "Earmark that, [Name]'s [specific thing]."
EARMARK AN IDEA      "Earmark that — an idea for [show/newsletter/product]: [the idea]."
CATCH TIPS IN TALK   Let people say "you have to try…" — name the thing.
PLANT A BLOOM        Same idea, 3 conversations, 3 different days.
AVOID                Trigger at the end · no name · two items in one note
CONSENT              Tell people the Bee is on.
```
