# 009 — Triage stays on Jev, with work-talk rules and tighter confidence lines

**Decision:** Triage keeps running on Jev 1.13 (`typesafe-ai/jev-1.13.0`). Its instructions now say plainly that naming a tool, company or product while talking about work is not a recommendation. An item is auto-accepted only when Jev is more than 90% sure (was 85%), dropped when it is under 60% sure (was 50%), and anything in between goes to review, where it shows in the garden as unconfirmed.

**Why this came up:** Every stretch of conversation Bee records passes through this gate. Too loose and the garden fills with things nobody recommended; too strict and real tips disappear. The PRD (the product requirements document) set targets of 85% precision for recommendations and 75% for ideas. Precision means: of the things Earmark files as a recommendation, how many really were one. The first eval on 48 of Tarik's real, hand-labeled conversation snippets (2026-10-17) showed Jev far below both targets. The biggest single failure was work talk: when colleagues discussed tools, companies or plans, Jev read "naming a thing" as "recommending a thing". Of the snippets Tarik labeled as neither recommendation nor idea, it got only 16% right; it called 30 runs a recommendation and 22 an idea. Jev reads instructions literally (its own documentation says so), so the fix belongs in the wording first, and only then in the numbers.

**Options:**
1. **Fix the wording, keep Jev, tune the lines from the eval.** Add exact conditions and made-up boundary examples to the instructions ("we exported the chart from Datawrapper" is neither; "you should make your charts in Datawrapper" is a recommendation), re-run the eval, then pick the lines. Cost: about 3 cents of model calls per run, and lines picked from 48 snippets can be wrong for new kinds of conversation.
2. **Switch to OpenAI Decisions** (`openai/gpt-6-luna-decisions`). It was much better at leaving work talk alone (73% right before the fix, 87% after). Cost: it is worse at the thing that matters. After the fix its idea precision was 36% against Jev's 55%. It never called anything a personal recommendation, so it also missed the one real one in the set. It costs about 2.4 times as much per call. That is pennies either way, so cost did not decide this.
3. **Keep the PRD's starting lines (85% / 50%) and change nothing.** Cost: no measurement behind them, and the eval shows Jev's guesses between 50% and 60% sure were almost all wrong.

**What we chose and why:** Option 1. Claude decided from the eval data; Tarik is informed. After the wording fix, Jev's overall accuracy rose from 53.5% to 69.4%, work-talk accuracy from 16% to 49%, recommendation precision from 7% to 13%, and idea precision from 41% to 55%. Its calibration error (ECE: how far stated confidence drifts from how often it is actually right; 0 is perfect) fell from 0.18 to 0.09. Decisions does not beat Jev on both recommendation and idea precision, which was the bar for switching, so Jev stays.

The lines:
- **Drop below 0.6.** This is the best-supported number in the eval. In the re-run, Jev's recommendation or idea calls under 60% sure were right 1 time in 21. In the first run they were right 0 times in 36.
- **Auto-accept above 0.9.** Above 0.9, Jev's answers of every kind were 97% right (36 answers, most of them "neither"). For real recommendations and ideas, though, only a handful of answers landed that high: 3 of 3 right above 0.85, 2 of 2 above 0.9. That is too few to measure. The tuning helper (`pnpm eval:tune`) suggested 0.85, resting on 3 answers. We rounded up to 0.9 on purpose: a wrong auto-accept looks confirmed in the garden, while a wrong review item is clearly marked as unconfirmed (decision 004).

**What we gave up:** Most real ideas and recommendations will now land in review, not auto-accept. In this eval, real ideas mostly scored between 0.6 and 0.7, and almost no idea scored above 0.9. Review-band ideas still count toward a bloom (decision 004), so the garden still grows, but more of it says "unconfirmed". Raising the drop line also means a rare real item scored at 55% is thrown away: one idea in this eval scored 0.53 on one run and 0.65 on another. The numbers rest on 48 snippets of mostly one person's speech, and only one of them was a real recommendation to Tarik. So the recommendation precision figures are anecdotes, not measurements, and the lines may not hold for other rooms or other speakers. Jev's answers also changed with the order of the options 17% of the time (up from 15%), which is above the 10% target.

**Impact on what is already saved (estimate only, nothing was re-processed):** If the current production items were re-routed on their stored confidences alone: 7 of 34 recommendations (all now in review) and 17 of 48 idea mentions (all now in review) fall under 0.6 and would be dropped. 2 auto-accepted recommendations and 7 auto-accepted idea mentions sit between 0.85 and 0.9 and would move to review. Re-triaging with the new wording would also change some of the kinds themselves; that cannot be estimated without new model calls on production data, so it was not done. Whether to re-process is Tarik's call.

**How we'll know if this was right:** (1) Re-run `pnpm eval:triage --yes` once there are at least 10 snippets labeled as a recommendation to Tarik and 10 labeled as an idea. Above 0.9, recommendation precision should be at least 85% and idea precision at least 75%. Below 0.6, positives should still be mostly wrong. (2) Over the first week, fewer than 1 in 5 garden items get dismissed as "not an idea / not a rec" (the PRD success metric). (3) The Slack review queue stays under about 5 items a day; if it is swamped, the 0.9 line is too strict.
