# 047 — Discogs adds label, year, format and credits to music tips, fetched once with the tip page's extras

**Decision:** Matched albums, songs and artists get a "Credits & label" section on their tip page (label and catalog number, year, format, genres and styles, and who played, wrote or produced), taken from Discogs (a large, volunteer-edited database of music releases). It is looked up once, by the same background step that already fetches the tip page's extras (decision 045), saved on the tip, and shown with the "Data provided by Discogs" credit that Discogs requires. A script fills it in for tips matched before today.

**Why this came up:** Decision 012 set the order for music lookups: MusicBrainz first (identifies the album), Apple Music as an optional extra, Discogs later, and the code has had a marked spot for it since. "Later" is now: for a radio programmer the label, the catalog number and the credits (who produced it, who played on it) are the facts that make a recommendation usable on air or worth chasing. What was at stake: a wrong match would put the wrong label or the wrong people on a record, which is worse than showing nothing; and Discogs limits callers to 60 requests a minute and has published terms we have to follow.

**Options:**
1. **Skip Discogs.** Cost: tip pages for music stay thin; nothing about labels or credits.
2. **Look it up at match time, next to the Apple Music step.** Cost: two to four extra Discogs calls on every music tip's critical path, and that step swallows errors by design, so a "slow down" answer would be hidden rather than obeyed.
3. **Look it up in the extras step, and backfill old tips with a script** (chosen). Cost: credits show up a few minutes after a tip is matched, like the other extras, and old tips need one manual script run.

**What we chose and why:** Option 3. Tarik chose to add Discogs (using a consumer key and secret, not OAuth; OAuth is only for acting on a person's own Discogs account, which Earmark never does). Claude chose where it plugs in. The extras step already has exactly the behaviour Discogs needs: a "slow down" answer (429) stops the step without counting against the tip, any other failure counts a try (three tries, then it stops asking), and the result is stored once. Doing it there also lets the refresh script reuse the same lookup. Matching is deliberately strict: the release's artist and title are each compared on their own and must equal ours once punctuation, case and accents are ignored (so "Prince" never matches "Prince Buster"; Discogs' own "(2)" suffix on same-named artists is ignored); two artists with the same name means no match; a song must really appear in the release's track list. If nothing matches confidently, nothing is stored. Calls are kept at least 1.1 seconds apart. Discogs' images are never read or stored: its terms treat them as restricted, while titles, dates, formats, credits, labels and artist names are public-domain (CC0) data.

**What we gave up:** Song credits come from the first Discogs release that holds the track by that artist, which may be a compilation, remix or bootleg rather than the original album. Tips whose extras were fetched while the Discogs keys were unset (or while Discogs was failing) are not revisited by the background step; run `pnpm refresh:music-addons` once the keys exist. Discogs trouble never costs a tip its other extras: a failure is logged and Discogs is left off, to be filled in by the script. Another outside dependency, and a limit of 60 requests a minute (fine at a few tips a day, slow for a big backfill: the script is sequential and can be run in batches with `--limit`). Strict matching misses real matches: compilations, "feat." titles, artists credited as "A & B", albums with no Discogs master. The stored data can drift from Discogs (each entry records when it was fetched; Discogs' terms call the data "dynamic" and ask apps not to keep it longer than needed, which we read as satisfied by it being CC0 data kept for one private owner, but it is a judgment, not a certainty). A tip with credits now shows a short trademark notice on the page.

**How we'll know if this was right:** Run the refresh script (dry run first), then open ten music tips Tarik knows well. Expect most albums with a Discogs master to show the right label and credits, and zero wrong ones; one wrong label means tightening the match. Count tips that got nothing: if more than half of albums miss, add a second search by release instead of master.
