# 029 — Serendipity picks by age, lives on Home, rests ideas and composts tips

**Decision:** Serendipity (FR-34) is a quiet section at the bottom of Home that shows one thing Tarik hasn't come back to in a while: a tip still waiting to be tried, or an idea that stopped growing. For a **tip** he taps **Keep it** or **Let it compost** (the tip is composted, which also removes its Bee todo, with an **Undo** for an hour). For an **idea** he taps **Keep it** or **Not now**: Not now *rests* the idea for 60 days. It is never dismissed; it stays in the garden and keeps growing if he mentions it again. The pick is made by age, with no AI model and no scheduled job.

**Why this came up:** Earmark keeps everything, so old tips and half-grown ideas quietly pile up below the newest ones. Serendipity brings one back at a time. The first build treated "let it compost" the same for both kinds, which dismissed an idea and took it out of the garden. Tarik ruled against that (2026-10-17, "Rest it"): an idea he isn't ready for today may be the one he needs in two months, and it is the lead feature of the product. A tip is different: composting it is a real verdict that he doesn't want it.

**Options:**
1. **Pick by age; on Home; ideas rest and tips compost** (chosen). Cost: the pick is plain ("the oldest thing you haven't touched"), not clever, and a rested idea can't be woken early from Serendipity.
2. **Dismiss ideas too, with Undo** (the first build). Cost: one tap removes an idea from the garden; after the hour there is no way back from the garden.
3. **Pick by meaning:** use embeddings (a way to find items that *mean* something similar) to bring back an old item related to something heard this week. Cost: more code, a judgment that can be wrong, and an item can feel random when the link is weak. Still open.
4. **A separate /serendipity page or a sixth tab.** Cost: the tab bar is already at five (Home, Garden, Shelf, People, Spaces), and a page you must remember to visit defeats the point of something resurfacing on its own.

**What we chose and why:** Option 1. Tarik made the rest-vs-dismiss call; Claude chose the rest. Age alone is enough while real history only starts Oct 15: a tip counts as forgotten after 3 days, an idea as stalled after 4 days without a new mention, and anything Serendipity showed waits at least a day before it can come back (week-one numbers; raise to 14 / 21 / 7 days after a month). A tip is offered only while it is "new" or "queued". An idea is offered only while it is in the compost or sprouting stage, not pinned, and not resting. Ideas that are confirmed or heard twice come before weak ones (unconfirmed, one mention), which only show when nothing stronger is waiting. That is a preference, not a filter. Composting a tip goes through the one place every status change goes (the status core), so the Bee todo is cleared the same way as anywhere else. Its Undo only works within the hour and only for a tip Serendipity composted, so it can never revive a tip composted somewhere else. Resting needed one new column, `idea_clusters.resting_until` (migration 040, additive). Forget (the delete-everything command) deletes the row it sits on, so it needs no change.

**What we gave up:** The pick has no sense of what Tarik is thinking about this week. A rested idea is out of Serendipity for 60 days even if he changes his mind (it is still in the garden and still grows). A weak idea can wait a long time if stronger ones keep coming. With the week-one thresholds, a tip saved on Monday can come back on Thursday, which may feel early.

**How we'll know if this was right:** Over the first two weeks, count the taps. If Keep it and Not now are both used on ideas, the item is worth deciding on. If he nearly always taps Not now, the pick is surfacing ideas too early or too weak. If he composts most tips, the shelf has noise upstream. If he ignores the section (the same item sits for days), the pick is too plain and option 3 is worth building.
