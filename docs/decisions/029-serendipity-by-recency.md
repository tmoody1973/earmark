# 029 — Serendipity picks by age alone, lives on Home, and composting can be undone

**Decision:** Serendipity (FR-34) is a quiet section at the bottom of Home that shows one thing Tarik hasn't come back to in a while: a tip still waiting to be tried, or an idea that stopped growing. He taps **Keep it** (it stays where it is and goes to the back of the line) or **Let it compost** (a tip is composted, which also removes its Bee todo; an idea is dismissed). Composting shows an **Undo** right away. The pick is made by age alone, with no AI model and no scheduled job.

**Why this came up:** Earmark keeps everything, so old tips and half-grown ideas quietly pile up below the newest ones. Serendipity brings one back at a time so Tarik either recommits or lets it go. Three choices could have gone differently: how to choose the item, where it lives, and whether "compost" is final. Getting "compost" wrong is the costly one: it removes a Bee todo and takes an idea out of the garden, so a slip of the thumb must not lose anything.

**Options:**
1. **Pick by age; show it on Home; Undo after composting** (chosen). Cost: the pick is plain ("the oldest thing you haven't touched"), not clever.
2. **Pick by meaning:** use embeddings (a way to find items that *mean* something similar) to bring back an old item related to something heard this week. Cost: more code, a judgment that can be wrong, and an item can feel random when the link is weak. Embeddings exist already, so this stays open.
3. **A separate /serendipity page, or a sixth tab.** Cost: the tab bar is already at five (Home, Garden, Shelf, People, Spaces), and a page you must remember to visit defeats the point of something resurfacing on its own.
4. **Compost with a confirm dialog instead of Undo.** Cost: a question before every tap, for an action that is easy to reverse.

**What we chose and why:** Option 1 (Claude's call, following the brief and the five-tab rule). Age alone is enough when real history starts on Oct 15: a tip counts as forgotten after 3 days, an idea as stalled after 4 days without a new mention, and anything Serendipity showed (kept or composted) waits at least a day before it can come back. Those are week-one numbers; they should rise to 14 / 21 / 7 days after a month of data. A tip is offered only while it is "new" or "queued" (never tried, thanked, unsure, dismissed or composted). An idea is offered only while it is in the compost or sprouting stage and not pinned; a ready idea already leads the garden, and drafted, used and dismissed ideas never move. Composting a tip goes through the one place every status change goes (the status core), so the Bee todo is cleared the same way as anywhere else. Undo restores a tip's old status; an idea regrows by the same rule that sets its stage from its mention count, and only if Serendipity dismissed it in the last hour, so an idea dismissed any other way, or merged into another, stays put. No new table or column was needed: both tables already had a `last_surfaced_at` column, so Forget (the delete-everything command) already covers it.

**What we gave up:** The pick has no sense of what Tarik is thinking about this week, so it can bring back something that doesn't fit the moment. Undo lasts only until he leaves the page (and, for an idea, only an hour); after that a composted tip or dismissed idea has no button to bring it back from the garden. "Let it compost" dismisses an idea, so the idea's own page then says "You dismissed this idea," which is a slightly different word for the same act. With the week-one thresholds, a tip saved on Monday can come back on Thursday, which may feel early.

**How we'll know if this was right:** Over the first two weeks, count the taps. If Keep and Let it compost are both used, the item is worth deciding on. If Tarik nearly always composts, the thresholds are too short or the shelf is full of noise. If he ignores the section (the same item sits there for days), the pick is too plain and option 2 is worth building. If he uses Undo often, the button labels are not clear enough.
