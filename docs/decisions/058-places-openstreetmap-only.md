# 058 — Places come only from OpenStreetMap, and place tips get details (hours, website, phone) like music and film do

**Decision:** Earmark stops using Google Places. Every place tip is looked up on OpenStreetMap (through Nominatim, its free "type a name, get a place" search), and a matched place gets a "The place" section on its page: what kind of place it is, its hours with "Open now", website, phone, menu, a reservations link, and things like takeaway or outdoor seating. All of it comes from OpenStreetMap, is fetched once, and is stored, like the film details (decision 056).

**Why this came up:** A pre-submission compliance check read Google's current terms (Places API policies and the Service Specific Terms, both updated October 2026). They allow storing only a place's ID indefinitely, coordinates for at most 30 days, and nothing else (no name, no address). Results shown on a map must be on a Google map, and Google data shown without one needs a "Google Maps" credit. Earmark broke all four: it kept Google's name, address, coordinates and link forever, drew Google's coordinates on an OpenStreetMap tile, and credited no one. Four of the eight real place tips held Google data, and six kept lists of Google options for "Which one?". The repo and a demo site are public during judging. At the same time, Tarik asked why places don't get details the way music and film do.

**Options:**
1. **OpenStreetMap only** (chosen). Cost: OpenStreetMap misses some small or new businesses that Google knows (in a test on our real tips it found Kawa Ramen, both locations, 1033 Omakase and Cinnabon, but missed Diaspora). Its details are entered by volunteers, so they can be missing or wrong (Kawa Ramen's Saturday hours read "11:30–09:30").
2. **Keep Google, follow its rules.** Store place IDs only, refresh coordinates before 30 days, fetch the address and any details on every page view, replace the map tile with a Google map image, and add "Google Maps" credits. Cost: half a day or more, a charge per page view at Google's most expensive tier for hours and photos, and Tarik turning on Google's Static Maps product. Gain: better coverage, plus ratings and photos.
3. **Leave it until after judging.** Cost: the app stays out of line with Google's terms while the public is looking.

**What we chose and why:** Option 1. Tarik chose it after the test showed OpenStreetMap already holds good details for our real places: Kawa Ramen's cuisine, hours, phone, website, reservations, takeaway, delivery and outdoor seating; 1033 Omakase's "reservations required" with its Resy link. OpenStreetMap's licence (ODbL) lets us store what we fetch as long as we credit "© OpenStreetMap contributors" wherever it shows, which the map tiles already do and the new section does too. Claude built it: one Nominatim lookup per matched place, sharing the existing one-request-per-second limit. Hours are kept only when Earmark understands them (the common forms like "Mo-Th 11:30-22:00; Fr,Sa 17:00-02:00") and no single stretch runs over 16 hours, so a typo is hidden rather than shown as fact. "Open now" is worked out when the page loads, in Milwaukee time. The four Google-matched tips are looked up again on OpenStreetMap, the stored Google option lists are cleared, and the Google key is removed from Vercel.

**What we gave up:** Coverage of places OpenStreetMap doesn't know (Diaspora loses its map until a link is pasted with Fix this tip). Ratings and photos. Hours in a fancier form (public holidays, seasons) are not shown at all. "Open now" assumes the place is in Milwaukee's time zone. Details are fetched once and never refreshed, so changed hours go stale.

**How we'll know if this was right:** After two weeks of real use, count place tips that matched versus total: if fewer than two in three match, revisit option 2. And count matched places whose page shows at least one detail (kind, hours, website or phone).
