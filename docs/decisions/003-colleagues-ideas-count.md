# 003 — Ideas from colleagues count in the compost

**Decision:** An idea said by anyone in a conversation Tarik is part of — not only by Tarik — goes into his idea compost and can bloom.

**Why this came up:** Bee labels every speaker "Unknown" in its transcripts, so Earmark cannot tell whether "what if we did a segment on…" came from Tarik or from a colleague in the same meeting. We had to choose what the idea compost means when the speaker is unknowable. Getting it wrong either fills the garden with noise or makes it miss real ideas.

**Options:**
1. **Keep every idea heard** ("ideas in my orbit"). Cost: some ideas in the garden won't be Tarik's own; a one-pager might be drafted for a coworker's pitch.
2. **Only Tarik's ideas**, guessed from first-person cues ("I think we should…", "my idea is…"). Cost: misses many of Tarik's own ideas (people rarely say "my idea"), and still lets through colleagues who talk in the first person — so it is less accurate, not more.
3. **Ask Tarik on every idea** (a Slack "yours or theirs?" card). Cost: a question per idea; breaks "organize nothing".

**What we chose and why:** Option 1 — Tarik's call, on Claude's recommendation (2026-10-16). An idea that comes up three times in his meetings is worth a one-pager no matter who said it first, and option 2's cue-guessing is less accurate than it sounds. The criteria Jev uses already say "a speaker proposes a new idea", so no code changed.

**What we gave up:** The garden is "ideas I keep hearing", not strictly "ideas I had". Credit for an idea's origin is not tracked.

**How we'll know if this was right:** In the M2 eval over Tarik's real transcripts, count how many accepted ideas came from someone else; and over the first week of use, how many bloomed ideas Tarik dismisses as "not mine, not useful". If that dismiss rate is above the PRD's 20% target, revisit.
